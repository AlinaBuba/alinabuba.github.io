FAQ

What happens in the first session?

We get to know each other, I ask about your request and life context, explain how the work goes and we agree on goals. You decide how much to share. After the session you can decide whether to continue.

Are sessions confidential?

Yes. I do not disclose information about you to third parties. Exceptions are provided by law, for example a direct threat to your life or health or that of others. I will explain this beforehand.

How many sessions will I need?

It depends on the request. Often noticeable changes appear within a few weeks of regular work, but timelines and results cannot be guaranteed. We will regularly review together whether the work helps.

Is online therapy effective?

Research shows that for many requests the online format works as well as in-person. You only need a stable internet connection and a place where no one can overhear you.

How is a psychologist different from a psychiatrist?

A psychiatrist is a medical doctor: makes medical diagnoses and can prescribe medication. A psychologist works through conversation, skills and support, and does not prescribe medication. If you need a psychiatrist, I will tell you.

What if I cannot attend?

Let me know at least 24 hours ahead and the session can be rescheduled at no cost. Later cancellations count as a held session.

What is CBT and what does a neuropsychological perspective mean?

CBT (cognitive-behavioural therapy) helps you notice how thoughts affect emotions and actions, and gradually change what gets in the way. A neuropsychological perspective means I also look at sleep, stress, workload and concentration.
